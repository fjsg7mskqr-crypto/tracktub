Ask a room full of hosts whether they drain the hot tub after every guest and you'll start an argument. One BiggerPockets thread on exactly this question has a host saying "[Absolutely ZERO guests wants to hear that you did not drain](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/530/topics/1212468-hot-tub-management-complete-drain-and-refills-after-guests)" and, a few replies later, another saying "No, it doesn't need to be drained every time." Both have a point. They're answering different questions.

**Short answer:** for most rental tubs, no. Water that's been tested, balanced and sanitized between stays is in better shape than fresh fill water that nobody has checked. What makes a tub safe is the sanitizer in the water and the routine behind it, not how recently the water came out of a hose. But there are real times when draining is the right call, and a schedule based on how much the tub gets used, not just a date on the calendar.

## Why so many hosts drain every time

It's mostly about how it looks to the guest. A guest can't see a sanitizer reading. They can see a freshly filled tub, and "we drain it after every stay" is an easy line to put in a listing.

There are practical reasons too. If the person doing the turnover isn't comfortable testing and adjusting chemistry, a full drain feels like a reset button. In the same BiggerPockets thread, one host prices it out: around $50 for a standard clean between bookings versus around $150 for a full drain, scrub and refill. Draining also means hours of reheating before the next check-in, and a cold tub at arrival is its own complaint.

## What actually keeps the water safe

Fresh water isn't sanitized water. A tub that's drained and refilled an hour before check-in has had very little time for the sanitizer to do its job, and if nobody tested it, nobody knows where it stands.

The CDC's guidance for [vacation rental owners and managers](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/hospitality/considerations-for-vacation-rental-owners-and-managers.html) puts the weight on disinfectant, not drain frequency. It tells owners to "monitor and maintain adequate disinfectant levels, even when the hot tub isn't in use," and to follow the manufacturer's recommendations for cleaning and for replacing the filter and water. It also points out that local laws may differ from CDC recommendations, so check what your county or state requires.

There's a cautionary example in a 2024 CDC report on [private balcony hot tubs on cruise ships](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7342a3.htm). Those tubs were supposed to be shock-chlorinated, drained and refilled weekly or between occupancies. Legionella was still found in six of 10 tubs on one ship and all eight on another, and the report notes the tubs ran with no residual disinfectant. Draining often didn't make up for water that wasn't being sanitized.

A rental hot tub closer to home makes the same point. In a [2026 CDC report on a New York rental property](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/wr/mm7522a1.htm), the hot tub was linked to two cases of Legionnaires' disease, and health officials learned it "was not regularly disinfected."

A note on numbers: the target range for chlorine or bromine depends on whose guidance you follow. Consumer spa guidance and CDC guidance for rentals and public tubs don't agree, so this post doesn't name one. The [CDC vacation rental page](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/hospitality/considerations-for-vacation-rental-owners-and-managers.html) lists its ranges, your tub's manual lists the manufacturer's, and your local health rules may set their own. Pick your standard deliberately, and test against it every visit.

## A drain schedule based on use, not the calendar

Manufacturers usually give a time-based answer. Bullfrog, for example, says to [drain and refill approximately every three to four months](https://www.bullfrogspas.com/draining-cleaning-refilling/), depending on usage, water quality and maintenance. That's written for a household tub.

A rental doesn't get household use. Different people every few days, sunscreen, lotion, swimsuits washed in detergent, and the guest who skipped the shower all add up faster. The CDC's [hot tub module](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/toolkit/hot-tub-module.html), written for public and commercial tubs, gives a usage-based formula instead:

> **Days between water changes = (tub volume in gallons ÷ 3) ÷ average users per day**

Run it for a typical rental. A 400-gallon tub used by an average of 4 people a day works out to about 33 days. The same tub used by 2 people a day works out to about 67 days. Base the average on a busy stretch, not a quiet one, round down, and shorten it further when you host big groups.

That's a starting point, not a rule. If your local regulations or the manufacturer say otherwise, they win.

## When draining is the right call

Whatever your schedule, drain (and scrub the shell while it's empty) when:

- **The schedule says so.** Use the formula above or the manufacturer's interval, whichever comes first for your usage.
- **The water won't come right.** If it stays cloudy, foamy or smelly after you've tested, balanced and sanitized properly, the water is often just worn out. More chemicals won't fix that.
- **Something got in it.** Vomit, feces, blood or heavy contamination after a party means a drain and a proper clean, not a top-up. Follow your local health department's instructions.
- **A guest reports illness linked to the tub.** Don't drain straight away. The CDC module says to drain "after collecting samples using public health instructions." Call your local health department first, and write down what you saw before you touch anything.
- **The tub sat unused.** A tub that's been off or unattended for a stretch should be checked carefully before guests use it again, and draining is often the simplest route.

The CDC module also says to scrub all surfaces each time the tub is drained. A drain without a scrub leaves biofilm on the shell and in the plumbing behind, ready to seed the new water.

## What to do between guests instead

If you're not draining every time, the between-guest visit has to do the real work. The full routine is in our [STR hot tub turnover checklist](/blog/str-hot-tub-turnover-checklist), but the core is short:

1. **Test first, before you add anything.** That's your "as found" reading. It tells you what the last guests did to the water.
2. **Adjust in order.** Balance first, then sanitizer, so the sanitizer can work.
3. **Clean what guests touch and see.** Wipe the waterline, skim, rinse the filter.
4. **Test again before you leave.** That's your "as left" reading, the state the next guest will find.
5. **Check the cover and lock.** Then take your photos.

Tested, sanitized water with a record behind it is a better answer to a guest's "was it cleaned?" than "we drained it", because you can show it. If a guest does complain, we cover what Airbnb asks for and what a good record looks like in [Guest says the hot tub was dirty](/blog/guest-says-hot-tub-was-dirty-airbnb-refunds-claims).

> **What to record on every visit**
>
> - Date and time, and who did the visit
> - Readings as found, before you add anything
> - Products added, and how much
> - Readings as left, after you're done
> - Filter rinse, and the date of the last drain and refill
> - Photos: the tub before you start, after you finish, and the cover on

The message usually comes in on the first night. The hot tub is dirty, or cloudy, or cold, or not working. Sometimes it's true. Sometimes the tub was fine at check-in and something happened after. Either way, you're about to have a conversation where the guest has photos from today and you have your memory of the last turnover.

This post walks through what Airbnb's own policy pages say about hot tub complaints, the deadlines for guests and for hosts, and what your record needs to show. It isn't legal advice, and Airbnb updates these pages, so read the current versions linked below before you act on a live case.

## A dirty or broken hot tub is a refund event

Airbnb's [Rebooking and Refund Policy](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3028) (effective February 6, 2025) lists what counts as a "Reservation Issue". Two items matter for hot tubs:

- A listing that is "not reasonably clean and sanitary" at check-in.
- An advertised amenity that "is not present or does not function", and the policy names hot tubs as an example, alongside pools and major appliances.

Guests must report Reservation Issues "within 72 hours after discovery." Airbnb may ask for evidence such as "photographs, videos, or confirmation of the conditions by the host."

Two more lines in that policy matter for hosts. How much gets refunded depends on several factors, including "the strength of evidence provided". And if Airbnb refunds the guest, the host will "have their payout reduced by the amount refunded."

Put simply: the guest's evidence is gathered during the stay, when the problem is in front of them. Unless you have your own record from before check-in, theirs is the only one.

## If the guest damaged the tub: the host claim clock

Sometimes the complaint runs the other way. Guests leave the tub half empty, overloaded with bubble bath, uncovered in a storm, or with a broken jet. Airbnb's [host damage protection](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/279) help page sets out the steps:

1. Document the issue with photos or videos, and get repair or cleaning estimates or receipts.
2. "Within 14 days of the responsible guest's checkout," file a reimbursement request in the Resolution Center.
3. The guest then gets 24 hours to respond. If they decline, don't respond, or pay only part, you can ask Airbnb to step in.

The full [Host Damage Protection Terms](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2869) go further. Evidence must show "the condition at the time of the loss or damage," and it must not be "doctored or falsified in any way", which the terms say includes using artificial intelligence. The terms also set a 30-day window for the formal request to Airbnb, but the help page's 14 days is the one to plan around.

Look at the exclusions too, because two of them land squarely on hot tubs:

- **Microorganisms.** The terms exclude losses involving bacteria, mold or "other microorganism," and anything that poses a threat to human health. If the water itself makes someone sick, host damage protection is not the safety net.
- **Wear and maintenance.** Loss of function "due to use, age, lack of maintenance" is excluded. A pump that failed because the tub wasn't looked after is your cost.

So in a damage claim, your record needs to show what the tub looked like _before_ the guest. In a maintenance or health question, it needs to show the tub was being looked after at all.

## What it looks like without a record

A host on the Airbnb Community forum describes a [case many will recognize](https://communitycenter.airbnb.com/forum/2d51a5c2-16fb-4016-833b-8f987dae813c/post/bGVnYWN5X2Nj_1874986). Before a New Year's stay, they had the tub cleaned, drained and refilled with fresh water, and a new pump put in. Mid-stay, the guests said the tub wasn't working. On a FaceTime call, the host saw it was "only half full with water and brown in color." The guests said the water had been displaced by the occupants. The host worried a refund request was coming. The thread never says how it ended.

The host may have done everything right. But the only evidence of the tub's condition was a video call made after the problem, controlled by the guest. There was no photo of a full, clear tub at the turnover, no water reading, and no time on anything.

## What a good hot tub record shows

Airbnb doesn't say what a host's hot tub record has to contain. From the policy language above, and from servicing rental tubs, these are the things that answer the questions that come up in a complaint:

- **The tub before you started, and after you finished.** One photo of the whole tub on arrival, and the same view when you leave, water level visible.
- **The cover on.** The last photo of the visit. It shows how the tub was left for the next guest.
- **When each photo was taken.** A pile of camera-roll photos can come from any day. A photo with the time attached ties it to this turnover.
- **The water as found and as left.** A reading before you add anything shows what the last guests left behind. A reading when you leave shows what the next guest got. If you only record one, record the "as left".
- **What you added.** Products and amounts. This shows a response, not just an observation.
- **Who did it, and that the record wasn't changed later.** A visit that's finished and closed is easier to rely on than notes someone can still edit.

Then, when a guest messages, pull the record for that check-in before you reply. Send the before and after photos with times, and the as-left reading. Keep it calm and factual. Say what the record _shows_. Don't promise it _proves_ anything, because Airbnb decides on its own read of everyone's evidence.

## What a record can't do

Be honest with yourself about the limits. A record shows the tub at the moment you left. It can't show what happened at 11 p.m. on night two. It doesn't override Airbnb's decision, and it won't help much if the readings in it are out of range. If a guest reports illness, stop and call your local health department before you clean or drain anything. The [CDC's guidance for vacation rental owners](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/hospitality/considerations-for-vacation-rental-owners-and-managers.html) is the place to start, and our post on [whether to drain between guests](/blog/drain-rental-hot-tub-between-guests) covers why you don't drain first.

The record's real job is to make the routine visible. The between-guest routine itself is in our [STR hot tub turnover checklist](/blog/str-hot-tub-turnover-checklist).

> **What to record on every visit**
>
> - A photo of the whole tub when you arrive, before you touch anything
> - Readings as found, before you add anything
> - Products added, and how much
> - Readings as left
> - A photo of the whole tub when you're done, water level visible
> - A photo with the cover on and locked
> - Anything broken or off, so the next visit sees it

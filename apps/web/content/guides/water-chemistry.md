Check four numbers on every visit: total alkalinity, pH, calcium hardness and free chlorine. For a rental tub, aim for alkalinity 80 to 120 ppm, pH 7.2 to 7.8, calcium hardness 150 to 250 ppm, and free chlorine 3 to 10 ppm. Adjust in that order, and test once as you find the water and once as you leave it.

Local health codes may be stricter than any number here. Check yours.

## The four numbers

- **Total alkalinity (TA)** is the water's buffer. It keeps pH from swinging.
- **pH** is how acidic the water is. It affects comfort and how well the sanitizer works.
- **Calcium hardness** protects the shell and equipment. Low hardness makes the water aggressive, and high hardness leads to scale.
- **Chlorine (or bromine)** is the sanitizer. This is the number that drops fastest in a tub that gets used a lot.

## Target ranges, and why sources disagree

| Number           | Target                           | Source for the range                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Total alkalinity | 80 to 120 ppm                    | [Leslie's](https://lesliespool.com/blog/spa-hot-tub-chemicals-for-dummies.html)                                                             |
| pH               | 7.2 to 7.8                       | [CDC, vacation rentals](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/hospitality/considerations-for-vacation-rental-owners-and-managers.html) |
| Calcium hardness | 150 to 250 ppm                   | [Leslie's](https://lesliespool.com/blog/spa-hot-tub-chemicals-for-dummies.html)                                                             |
| Free chlorine    | 3 to 10 ppm (bromine 4 to 8 ppm) | [CDC, vacation rentals](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/hospitality/considerations-for-vacation-rental-owners-and-managers.html) |

Sources do not all agree, and that is normal:

- **Chlorine.** The CDC's vacation rental page gives 3 to 10 ppm free chlorine. A consumer retailer such as [Leslie's](https://lesliespool.com/blog/spa-hot-tub-chemicals-for-dummies.html) gives 1 to 3 ppm for a home tub. We use the CDC's range because a rental tub is shared by strangers.
- **pH.** The CDC's rental page says 7.2 to 7.8. Its [guidance for public tubs](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/toolkit/operating-public-pools-hot-tubs-and-splash-pads.html) says 7.0 to 7.8, and Leslie's aims for 7.4 to 7.6. All of them overlap in the middle.
- **Alkalinity and hardness.** The CDC pages above do not give alkalinity or calcium hardness ranges, so these come from a retailer. Your tub's manufacturer may differ; follow the manual when it does.
- **Local codes.** Health departments can set their own numbers. If you are inspected or licensed, use theirs.

## Test order and adjust order

Test all four first. Then adjust in this order:

1. Total alkalinity
2. pH
3. Calcium hardness
4. Sanitizer

Alkalinity goes first because it buffers pH: correcting it often brings pH into range ([SpaDepot](https://spadepot.com/pages/test-strips)). Change one thing at a time, circulate the water, and retest before adding more. SpaDepot suggests circulating for a few hours or overnight before retesting after adding chemicals. On a same-day turnover, circulate as long as you can and say in your record if you retested sooner.

## Reading test strips without guessing

- **Check the strip measures what you need.** Some strips read total chlorine, which includes combined chlorine. The CDC range is for free chlorine, so a total-chlorine strip can read higher than the free chlorine really is ([SpaDepot](https://spadepot.com/pages/test-strips), [LaMotte](https://lamotte.com/article-and-insight/insta-test-test-strip-tips/)).
- **Use fresh strips.** Check the date on the bottle, keep it capped, and do not pull strips out with wet fingers.
- **Dip and read as the bottle says.** LaMotte suggests turning the jets off, reading all pads in natural daylight, and reading everything before 30 seconds have passed. They also say to read pH last, because it benefits from a few extra seconds.
- **Read the majority colour.** If a pad shows two colours, LaMotte says to use the one that covers most of it.
- **Wash your hands first.** Sanitizer on your fingers can skew results.

Different brands work differently. The bottle's instructions win.

## What heavy guest use does to the numbers

A rental tub can go from empty to full of people and back several times a week. People bring oils, lotions and sweat into the water, and sanitizer is used up as it deals with them. That is why the rental routine tests every visit instead of weekly. The CDC's advice to keep disinfectant up even when the tub is not in use also applies in the gap between stays.

We do not have measured data on how far chlorine drops between guests, so we are not putting a number on it here. Your own as-found readings will tell you what your tub does.

## As found vs. as left

Record two sets of readings: as found, before you touch anything, and as left, after you adjust. The first shows what the last stay did to the water. The second shows where you left it. The gap between them is what you did. If a number is still out of range when you leave, write it down and handle it first at the next visit.

A rental hot tub gets the same routine before every check-in: test the water as you find it, fix what is out of range, clean the shell and filter, check the cover and cabinet, test again, and photograph and record the result. The [Short Term Shop's hot tub guide](https://theshorttermshop.com/hot-tub-guide-short-term-rental/) puts the between-guest work at 15 to 20 minutes.

Rental tubs take heavy bather loads, often back to back. The [CDC's guidance for vacation rental owners](https://www.cdc.gov/control-legionella/php/hospitality/considerations-for-vacation-rental-owners-and-managers.html) is to monitor and maintain adequate disinfectant levels, even when the tub is not in use. Local rules may be stricter than the CDC's, so check yours.

## The 15-minute between-guest routine

Run the same steps in the same order every time, whether you or a cleaner does them:

1. Test the water as you found it.
2. Adjust, in order.
3. Wipe the shell and waterline, and rinse the filter.
4. Check the cover and cabinet.
5. Test again.
6. Photograph and record.

The sections below cover each step. For the numbers behind steps 1, 2 and 5, see the [water chemistry guide](/guides/water-chemistry).

## Test first: as found

Test before you touch anything, and write the numbers down. Four readings: alkalinity, pH, calcium hardness and chlorine (or bromine).

The as-found reading shows what the last guests did to the water. Once you add chemicals, that information is gone. It is also the reading you will want if someone later asks what state the tub was in.

## Adjust in the right order

Adjust alkalinity first, then pH, then calcium hardness, then sanitizer. Alkalinity buffers pH, so correcting it first often brings pH into range on its own ([SpaDepot](https://spadepot.com/pages/test-strips)).

Add products one at a time, circulate, and retest before adding more. If the tub was heavily used, shock it per the manufacturer's instructions. Cloudy or foamy water at check-in is the complaint guests notice first.

## Shell, waterline, filter rinse

- Wipe the waterline and shell to remove oils and film, and skim the surface.
- Pull the filter and rinse it. Replace or deep-clean it on the schedule in your manufacturer's manual.

A clogged filter strains the pump and can hide water problems.

## Cover and cabinet check

- Cover: intact, latches securely, and locks if your listing requires it.
- Cabinet: listen for unusual pump noise, look for leaks, and check the panel for error codes.
- Temperature: the [CDC's operating guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/toolkit/operating-public-pools-hot-tubs-and-splash-pads.html) says to keep water from exceeding 104°F (40°C).

## Test again: as left

When the water has circulated, test again. This is the as-left reading. If a number is still out of range, say so in your notes and carry it to the next visit instead of leaving the tub looking fine.

## What to photograph (before / after / cover on)

Take the same three photos every visit:

- **Before:** the whole tub as you found it, before you touch anything.
- **After:** the whole tub when you are done, with water level and clarity visible.
- **Cover on:** the tub closed up the way guests will find it.

Take them at the tub, with the phone camera, at the time of the visit. A photo that shows when it was taken is more useful than a pile of photos from unknown days.

## What to record

Whether you use paper or an app, the [CDC's toolkit](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/toolkit/operating-public-pools-hot-tubs-and-splash-pads.html) asks operators of public tubs to keep accurate records of disinfectant and pH measurements and maintenance activities. For a rental tub it is a good habit too. At minimum:

| Field                         | Why it matters                                            |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Date, time, who did the visit | Shows when the tub was last serviced relative to check-in |
| Readings as found             | Shows what the last stay did to the water                 |
| Products added, with amounts  | Shows what you did about it                               |
| Readings as left              | Shows where you left the water                            |
| Filter and drain dates        | Shows the longer-cycle upkeep                             |
| Open items                    | Makes sure a known problem is handled at the next visit   |

## When to drain instead

A routine visit does not need a drain (see [should you drain between every guest](/guides/drain-between-guests)). Drain and clean the tub when:

- The water stays cloudy or smells off after the chemistry is correct and the filter is clean.
- A guest reports illness or a rash. Document the water first (readings and photos), then follow your manufacturer's and local health department's guidance. Do not re-clean before you have recorded the state of the tub. For the complaint itself, see [guest says the hot tub was dirty](/guides/guest-says-tub-was-dirty).
- It is due. The Short Term Shop suggests draining and cleaning every 3 to 4 months, or more often with heavy use. Your manual may say otherwise.
